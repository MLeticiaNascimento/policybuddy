# 1. Introduction

GlobalTech Solutions recognizes information as one of its most valuable business assets. Protecting corporate information, customer data, intellectual property, and technology resources is essential to maintaining business continuity, customer trust, and regulatory compliance.

This Information Security Policy establishes the security principles, requirements, and responsibilities necessary to protect company information from unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, destruction, or misuse.

As a global organization operating in technology, healthcare, financial, and manufacturing environments, GlobalTech Solutions maintains a strong commitment to cybersecurity practices aligned with industry standards and risk management principles.

All employees, contractors, consultants, suppliers, and third parties who access company systems, applications, networks, or information assets must comply with the requirements defined in this policy.

Information security is a shared responsibility. Every individual who interacts with company information contributes to maintaining a secure business environment.

---

# 2. Policy Objective

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of company information.
- Establish security requirements for employees and third parties.
- Reduce cybersecurity risks and prevent security incidents.
- Define acceptable practices for the use of corporate technology resources.
- Ensure appropriate protection of confidential and restricted information.
- Promote awareness of cybersecurity threats and employee responsibilities.

GlobalTech Solutions applies the following security principles:

## Confidentiality

Information must only be accessible to authorized individuals with a legitimate business requirement.

## Integrity

Information must remain accurate, complete, and protected against unauthorized changes.

## Availability

Systems and information must be available to authorized users when required for business operations.

## Least Privilege

Users must receive only the minimum level of access necessary to perform their job responsibilities.

# 4. Information Classification

GlobalTech Solutions classifies information according to sensitivity and business impact.

Employees must understand the classification level of information they handle and apply appropriate protection measures.

---

# 4.1 Public Information

Public information is information approved for external disclosure.

Examples:

- Published marketing materials.
- Public website content.
- Press releases.
- Public job postings.

Protection requirements:

- Information may be shared externally after appropriate approval.
- Employees must ensure content accuracy before publication.

---

# 4.2 Internal Information

Internal information is company information intended for employees and authorized partners.

Examples:

- Internal procedures.
- Employee communications.
- Operational documents.
- Training materials.

Protection requirements:

- Must not be shared publicly.
- Access should be limited to employees with business needs.

---

# 4.3 Confidential Information

Confidential information contains sensitive business, customer, or operational data.

Examples:

- Customer contracts.
- Financial information.
- Business strategies.
- Employee records.
- Product development information.

Protection requirements:

- Access requires authorization.
- Sharing must follow approved processes.
- Storage must use approved corporate systems.

---

# 4.4 Restricted Information

Restricted information represents the highest level of sensitivity.

Examples:

- Authentication credentials.
- Security configurations.
- Encryption keys.
- Highly sensitive customer information.
- Unreleased business strategies.

Protection requirements:

- Strict access control.
- Additional security monitoring.
- Encryption when required.
- Sharing only with explicitly authorized individuals.

---

# 5. Access Control Requirements

## 5.1 Principle of Least Privilege

GlobalTech Solutions follows the principle of least privilege.

Employees must only receive access required to perform their assigned responsibilities.

Access requests must:

- Have a valid business justification.
- Be approved by appropriate managers.
- Follow established access management procedures.

Employees must not:

- Request unnecessary permissions.
- Access information unrelated to their responsibilities.
- Attempt to bypass security controls.

---

# 5.2 Account Management

Users are responsible for protecting their accounts.

Employees must:

- Use individual accounts.
- Keep credentials confidential.
- Report suspicious account activity.
- Lock devices when unattended.

Employees must not:

- Share passwords.
- Use another person's account.
- Allow unauthorized individuals to access company systems.

---

# 5.3 Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) must be enabled for systems requiring additional identity verification.

MFA may include:

- Authentication applications.
- Hardware tokens.
- Biometric verification.
- Additional security confirmation methods.

Users must not disable, bypass, or share MFA authentication methods.

---

# 6. Corporate Device Usage

Corporate devices are provided to support business activities and must be protected appropriately.

Examples include:

- Laptops.
- Mobile devices.
- Tablets.
- Workstations.

Employees must:

- Keep devices physically secure.
- Install required security updates.
- Use approved security tools.
- Report lost or stolen devices immediately.

Employees must not:

- Allow unauthorized users to use company devices.
- Remove security software.
- Modify security configurations without approval.

---

# 7. Personal Device Usage (BYOD)

The use of personal devices for accessing corporate information is restricted.

Personal devices may only be used when:

- Approved by the company.
- Security requirements are satisfied.
- Required protections are enabled.

Employees must not store confidential company information on personal devices.

Examples of prohibited activities:

- Saving customer files on personal computers.
- Using personal cloud storage for company documents.
- Downloading confidential information to personal devices.

---

# 8. Remote Work Security

Employees working remotely must maintain appropriate security practices.

Requirements include:

- Using approved company devices whenever possible.
- Protecting confidential conversations.
- Preventing unauthorized screen access.
- Following secure network requirements.

Employees must ensure that home environments provide adequate protection for company information.

Examples:

- Avoid leaving laptops unattended.
- Avoid discussing confidential topics where others can listen.
- Use privacy screens when required.

---

# 9. VPN Requirements

Employees accessing corporate systems remotely must use approved secure connections.

VPN usage is required when:

- Accessing internal company resources.
- Connecting from untrusted networks.
- Handling confidential information remotely.

Employees must not:

- Disable VPN security settings.
- Use unauthorized VPN solutions.
- Share VPN credentials.

---

# 10. Software Installation Policy

Only approved software may be installed on company devices.

Employees must:

- Use approved software catalogs.
- Request authorization for new applications.
- Follow security review procedures.

Employees must not install:

- Unauthorized applications.
- Pirated software.
- Unknown browser extensions.
- Tools that bypass security controls.

---

# 11. Cloud Storage Security

Corporate information must only be stored in approved cloud platforms.

Employees must not use personal cloud accounts for business information.

Examples of prohibited storage:

- Personal Google Drive accounts.
- Personal Dropbox accounts.
- Unapproved file-sharing websites.

Approved cloud platforms must:

- Apply access controls.
- Support security monitoring.
- Protect company information.

---

# 12. Phishing and Social Engineering Protection

Cybercriminals frequently use phishing and social engineering techniques to obtain unauthorized access.

Employees must remain alert to:

- Suspicious emails.
- Unexpected attachments.
- Fake login pages.
- Urgent requests for information.

Warning signs include:

- Requests for passwords.
- Unknown senders.
- Unusual payment requests.
- Links directing to unfamiliar websites.

Employees must report suspicious messages to the Security team.

# 13. Security Incident Examples

## Case 1: Unauthorized Software Installation

### Situation:

An employee downloads and installs free software from the internet without approval.

### Risk:

- Malware infection.
- Data exposure.
- Security vulnerabilities.

### Applicable Policy:

Information Security Policy – Software Installation Requirements.

### Required Action:

The employee must request approval before installing software.

The Security team may investigate the software and determine whether it can be approved.

---

# Case 2: Suspicious Email Link

### Situation:

An employee receives an email requesting immediate password verification and clicks the included link.

### Risk:

- Credential theft.
- Account compromise.
- Malware infection.

### Applicable Policy:

Information Security Policy – Phishing Protection.

### Required Action:

The employee must:

1. Report the email immediately.
2. Change credentials if requested by Security.
3. Follow incident response instructions.

Employees should not attempt to investigate suspicious links independently.

---

# Case 3: Lost Corporate Laptop

### Situation:

An employee loses a company laptop while traveling.

### Risk:

- Unauthorized access.
- Exposure of confidential information.
- Data compromise.

### Applicable Policy:

Information Security Policy – Corporate Device Protection.

### Required Action:

The employee must immediately:

1. Report the lost device to Information Security.
2. Provide incident details.
3. Follow device protection procedures.

The Security team may remotely disable access or perform security actions.
