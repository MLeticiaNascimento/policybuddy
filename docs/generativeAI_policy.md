# 1. Introduction

GlobalTech Solutions recognizes that Generative Artificial Intelligence (Generative AI) technologies provide significant opportunities to improve productivity, innovation, automation, and decision-making across business functions.

Artificial Intelligence tools can support employees in activities such as content creation, data analysis, software development assistance, research, and process optimization.

However, the use of Generative AI technologies introduces risks related to data privacy, intellectual property protection, information security, accuracy of generated content, and regulatory compliance.

This Responsible Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence Policy establishes mandatory requirements for the appropriate, secure, and ethical use of AI-powered tools within GlobalTech Solutions.

All employees, contractors, consultants, and third parties using Generative AI technologies for business activities must comply with the requirements defined in this policy.

Employees remain responsible for all content created, reviewed, shared, or used with the support of Artificial Intelligence systems.

Artificial Intelligence tools must be considered assistants and not replacements for professional judgment, human review, or organizational accountability.

---

# 2. Policy Objective

The objectives of this policy are to:

- Establish guidelines for responsible use of Generative AI technologies.
- Protect confidential information and personal data.
- Prevent unauthorized disclosure of company information.
- Define acceptable and prohibited AI usage scenarios.
- Ensure AI-generated content receives appropriate human validation.
- Support ethical, secure, and compliant AI adoption.

GlobalTech Solutions promotes innovation while maintaining strong governance practices to reduce operational, legal, security, and reputational risks.

---

# 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

- All GlobalTech Solutions employees.
- Contractors.
- Consultants.
- Temporary workers.
- Third parties accessing company information.

This policy covers the use of:

- Public AI chatbots.
- Internal AI assistants.
- Generative AI platforms.
- AI coding assistants.
- AI content generation tools.
- AI-powered productivity applications.

Examples of Generative AI usage include:

- Text generation.
- Code generation.
- Image generation.
- Data summarization.
- Document analysis.
- Automated recommendations.

# 4. Definition of Generative AI

Generative Artificial Intelligence refers to systems capable of creating new content based on user-provided instructions or existing data patterns.

Examples include:

- AI chat assistants.
- Large Language Models (LLMs).
- AI programming assistants.
- Content generation platforms.

Generative AI systems may generate:

- Text.
- Software code.
- Images.
- Reports.
- Summaries.
- Recommendations.

Employees must understand that AI-generated outputs may contain inaccuracies, incomplete information, or unintended content.

---

# 5. Approved Use of Generative AI

Employees may use Generative AI tools for approved business activities when company requirements are followed.

Examples of acceptable use include:

---

## 5.1 Creating Draft Content

Employees may use AI tools to create initial drafts of:

- Internal communications.
- Meeting summaries.
- Non-confidential documentation.
- General business content.

Example:

An employee asks an approved AI assistant to help improve the structure of a presentation containing publicly available information.

The employee remains responsible for reviewing the final content.

---

## 5.2 Public Information Research

Employees may use Generative AI for research involving publicly available information.

Examples:

- Summarizing public articles.
- Generating ideas.
- Understanding general concepts.
- Creating learning materials.

Employees must verify information before using AI-generated research in business decisions.

---

## 5.3 Productivity Assistance

Approved AI tools may be used to support productivity activities.

Examples:

- Improving writing quality.
- Creating task checklists.
- Generating meeting agendas.
- Organizing non-confidential information.

---

## 5.4 Software Development Assistance

AI coding assistants may be used when approved by the organization.

Acceptable activities include:

- Generating sample code.
- Explaining programming concepts.
- Improving code readability.
- Creating documentation.

Developers must review AI-generated code before implementation.

AI-generated code must comply with:

- Security requirements.
- Licensing requirements.
- Software development standards.

---

# 6. Prohibited Use of Generative AI

Employees must not use public or unauthorized AI tools to process confidential, restricted, or personal information.

The following activities are strictly prohibited:

---

# 6.1 Uploading Customer Information

Employees must not enter:

- Customer names.
- Customer records.
- Personal information.
- Customer contracts.
- Customer reports.

into public AI platforms.

Example:

An employee uploads a customer database into a public chatbot to generate analysis.

This action is prohibited because customer information may be exposed or processed outside company-controlled environments.

---

# 6.2 Uploading Proprietary Source Code

Employees must not submit:

- Internal application code.
- Proprietary algorithms.
- Software architecture information.
- Security-related code.

to unauthorized AI tools.

Risk:

- Intellectual property exposure.
- Loss of competitive advantage.
- Security vulnerabilities.

---

# 6.3 Uploading Confidential Contracts

Employees must not upload:

- Legal agreements.
- Supplier contracts.
- Customer contracts.
- Confidential business documents.

into public AI services.

Contract information may contain sensitive commercial or legal information.

---

# 6.4 Sharing Strategic Information

Employees must not provide AI systems with:

- Business strategies.
- Financial projections.
- Acquisition plans.
- Product roadmaps.
- Internal investigations.

unless the AI solution has been formally approved.

---

# 7. Use of Public AI Tools

Public AI tools are services available externally and not managed by GlobalTech Solutions.

Examples:

- Public AI chatbots.
- Free AI writing tools.
- Public code-generation platforms.

Before using public AI tools, employees must confirm:

- The tool is approved.
- Information entered does not contain confidential data.
- Company security requirements are followed.

Employees must assume that information submitted to public AI tools may not remain private unless explicitly guaranteed by an approved agreement.

---

# 8. Approved AI Tools and Application Review Process

New AI tools must undergo evaluation before being used for business purposes.

The approval process may include review by:

- AI Governance.
- Information Security.
- Compliance.
- Legal Department.
- Data Privacy teams.

The evaluation may consider:

- Data protection capabilities.
- Security controls.
- Vendor reputation.
- Data storage location.
- Privacy practices.
- Regulatory requirements.

Employees must not independently adopt AI tools for processing corporate information without approval.

---

# 9. Human Responsibility and Validation

Artificial Intelligence does not replace employee accountability.

Employees are responsible for:

- Reviewing AI-generated content.
- Confirming accuracy.
- Identifying potential bias.
- Ensuring compliance with policies.
- Validating decisions.

Employees must not rely blindly on AI-generated recommendations.

Examples requiring human review:

- Legal documents.
- Financial reports.
- Customer communications.
- Security decisions.
- Employee evaluations.

---

# 10. AI Generated Content Accuracy

Generative AI systems may produce incorrect or misleading information.

Employees must verify:

- Facts.
- Calculations.
- References.
- Technical recommendations.

AI-generated information must not be considered automatically accurate.

Example:

An employee uses AI to generate a compliance report.

Before distribution, the employee must verify that all statements are correct and supported by reliable sources.

---

# 11. Data Privacy Requirements

When using AI tools, employees must follow data protection principles.

Employees must:

- Minimize information shared with AI systems.
- Avoid entering personal data unnecessarily.
- Use approved AI environments.
- Follow privacy requirements.

Employees must not:

- Upload employee records.
- Upload health information.
- Upload customer personal data.

unless specifically authorized.

---

# 12. AI Security Risks

Improper AI usage may create security risks, including:

- Information leakage.
- Intellectual property exposure.
- Unauthorized data processing.
- Malware generation.
- Incorrect business decisions.

Employees must immediately report suspected AI-related security incidents.

Examples:

- Accidental upload of confidential information.
- Unauthorized AI tool usage.
- Exposure of company data.

---

# 13. Example Scenarios

## Case 1: Creating a Presentation Draft

### Situation:

An employee uses an approved AI assistant to create a first draft of a presentation containing publicly available market information.

### Analysis:

This is acceptable because:

- No confidential information was provided.
- The AI tool is approved.
- The employee reviews the final content.

### Required Action:

Review and validate the generated material before use.

---

# Case 2: Customer Data Uploaded to AI Tool

### Situation:

An employee uploads a customer report into a public AI chatbot to generate insights.

### Risk:

- Customer data exposure.
- Privacy violation.
- Unauthorized data processing.

### Applicable Policy:

- Responsible Use of Generative AI Policy.
- Data Protection and Privacy Policy.

### Required Action:

The employee must report the incident immediately to Information Security.

---

# Case 3: Using AI to Generate Production Code

### Situation:

A developer uses an AI coding assistant to generate application code.

### Analysis:

This may be acceptable if:

- The tool is approved.
- No proprietary code was exposed.
- Security review requirements are followed.

### Required Action:

The developer must review, test, and validate the generated code before deployment.

Violations of this policy may result in:

- Mandatory AI security training.
- Removal of AI access.
- Formal warnings.
- Disciplinary actions.
- Termination of employment or contracts.
- Legal consequences when applicable.

Examples of violations:

- Uploading confidential data into unauthorized AI tools.
- Sharing proprietary information with public AI systems.
- Using AI to bypass security controls.
